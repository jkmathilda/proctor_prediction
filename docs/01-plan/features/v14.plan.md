## 1. Objective

Formalize the three-stage architecture already sketched (docstring-only, unimplemented) in the untracked
`scripts/model1.py` into a runnable `scripts/v14.py`, and replace that stub's currently-unverified
`data_analysis.ipynb` citations with numbers actually pulled from the notebook as it exists today.

Paradigm (from `model1.py`, carried forward):
1. **Stage 1** — global Ridge regression per target (regularized linear).
2. **Stage 2** — XGBoost fit on Stage 1's residuals, fold-isolated, heavier regularization than a
   direct-target model (residual signal-to-noise is much lower after Stage 1 removes the linear trend).
3. **Stage 3** — a low-capacity fine/coarse-grained correction (split on `fine-grained`) on top of the
   Stage 1+2 residual, sized to the small per-group row counts.
4. Final = linear + GBM-residual + group correction, saturation-clipped.
5. Evaluated by its own ablation (Stage 1 alone → Stage 1+2 → Stage 1+2+3), not benchmarked against v1–v13
   per `model1.py`'s explicit design intent — see §7 for why v5 is still reported alongside as context.

## 3. Fact-check: `model1.py`'s cited numbers vs. the actual notebook

`model1.py`'s docstring justifies Stage 1 (Ridge) with: *"data_analysis.ipynb cells 16–17 show a plain OLS
already reaches ~71–73% validation R² ... (0.78/0.71 MDD, 0.80/0.73 OWC)"*. Pulling the actual notebook
cells that compute this (positional cells 18 and 24 — the only OLS/Ridge CV tables in the notebook):

| Model | MDD train R² | MDD val R² | OWC train R² | OWC val R² | OWC val R² std |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Linear (cell 18) | 0.813 | 0.744 | 0.827 | **0.302** | 0.878 |
| Ridge (cell 24) | 0.798 | 0.735 | 0.812 | **0.388** | 0.736 |

MDD roughly matches the docstring's claim (~0.74 val R², stable). **OWC does not** — its val R² sits at
0.30–0.39, not 0.73, and its per-fold std is enormous (Linear fold 4 scores **R² = −1.45**, i.e. worse than
predicting the mean). The docstring's OWC figure appears to be simply wrong (possibly transcribed from the
wrong column), and this needs correcting before `v14.py` treats Ridge's OWC fit as reliable.

**Likely cause, and why it matters for v14's design**: cells 18/24 use a plain shuffled
`KFold(random_state=42)` — the exact fold-composition pathology v7 already diagnosed and fixed for a
different comparison (`soil_composition_strata()` in `scripts/v7.py`, `density_strata()` in `scripts/v13.py`):
at n=201, a random fold can by chance concentrate an unusual soil-composition mix, and a linear model is far
more exposed to that than a tree ensemble. Fold 4's catastrophic OWC score is consistent with this.

**Action for v14**: re-run Stage 1's CV with soil-composition- or density-stratified folds (reuse v7/v13's
existing implementation) before trusting any OLS/Ridge val R² number — don't carry the notebook's
plain-KFold figure forward as-is.

## 4. XGBoost baseline — quantitative support for Stage 2

Cell 49's Linear/Ridge/XGBoost comparison (same `params_av` feature set, same plain KFold):

| Model | MDD val R² | OWC val R² | OWC val std |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linear | 0.744 | 0.302 | 0.082 |
| Ridge | 0.735 | 0.388 | 0.035 |
| **XGBoost** | **0.835** | **0.797** | 0.047 |

XGBoost's gap over Ridge is dramatically larger for OWC (+0.41) than MDD (+0.10), and its fold-to-fold std
is an order of magnitude tighter than Linear's — this is real quantitative backing (not an assumption) for
`model1.py`'s premise that a nonlinear Stage 2 is essential, and confirms OWC is where nearly all of the
value from going nonlinear is concentrated.

**Feature importance is spread thin, not concentrated** (cell 52, XGBoost gain): the top engineered terms
are `feat_cc` (0.0485 MDD gain), `feat_cu` (0.0482), `feat_log_cu` (0.0266) — each a small share of total
gain. No single hand-engineered feature dominates, which argues for Stage 2 staying a full tree model over
the residual rather than trying to hand-pick a short list of residual predictors.

## 5. Stage 3 group-size check (open question, not yet resolved)

`data_split_by_soil.py`'s dominant-class counts (train, n=201): Sand=129, Gravel=45, Silt=26, **Clay=1**.
`model1.py` deliberately splits on the boolean `fine-grained` (`clay% + silt% > 12`) rather than dominant
soil class — this sidesteps the Clay=1 singleton problem a class-based split would hit, which is a sound
design choice. But the actual resulting group sizes under this specific boolean split haven't been computed
yet; `model1.py` itself flags this as an open empirical question (`diagnose_residuals_by_soil_group`). This
must be measured before finalizing how low-capacity Stage 3 needs to be — a 20/181 split needs a much
simpler correction than 80/121.

## 6. Proposed `v14.py` method

- **Stage 1** — `Ridge` (scaled), separate model per target.
- **Stage 2** — `XGBRegressor` on Stage 1 residuals, fold-isolated, tuned toward *more* shrinkage/regularization
  than v1's tuned XGBoost (lower `learning_rate`, higher `min_child_weight`/`reg_lambda`, shallower `max_depth`)
  since residual SNR is lower than direct-target SNR.
- **Stage 3** — split on `fine-grained`, per-group bias or 1-parameter scale correction only (not a second
  GBM) — capacity gated by the group-size check in §5.
- **Folds** — soil-composition- or density-stratified k-fold (reuse v7's/v13's existing implementation),
  not plain `KFold` — required by the §3 finding, not optional.
- **Physical clip** — reuse `helper_functions.calc_satline` (already validated equivalent to a hand-rolled
  ZAV formula since v5) rather than `model1.py`'s stated "own zav_density implementation" — no reason to
  reintroduce a second implementation of the same formula.
- **Evaluation** — report Stage-1-alone / Stage-1+2 / Stage-1+2+3 NMAE as the primary (ablation-only) gate,
  per `model1.py`'s explicit design; additionally report the final NMAE next to v5's 0.2410–0.2420 for
  project-level context, since that number is the actual thing being competed against regardless of this
  script's internal framing.

**Open question for Design phase**: `model1.py`'s docstring doesn't specify an imputation/feature-engineering
source. Options: (a) reuse `helper_functions.py`'s fold-isolated MICE pipeline, consistent with v5–v13's
precedent, or (b) the notebook's own simpler median-imputation set (v1–v4 precedent, closer to what
`model1.py`'s Stage 1 rationale was actually validated against in §3–4). Needs a decision before
implementation, not left implicit.
